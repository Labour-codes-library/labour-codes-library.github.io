What counts as wages

The Codes' definition of wages, what it leaves out, and the 50% rule that adds excluded pay back.

Written from the official text · not reviewed by a lawyer · follows the Central Rules

Key points

  • “Wages” means all remuneration, whether as salaries, allowances or otherwise, payable to a person in respect of their employment, and includes basic pay, dearness allowance and retaining allowance s. 2(y).
  • It leaves out, among other things, statutory bonus that is not part of the terms of employment, house accommodation and amenities, the employer’s pension or provident fund contributions, conveyance allowance, special expenses, house rent allowance, overtime allowance, commission, gratuity and retrenchment compensation s. 2(y)(a)–(k).
  • The 50% rule: if the excluded payments in clauses (a) to (i) are more than one-half of all remuneration, the amount above one-half is treated as remuneration and added to wages s. 2(y), first proviso.
  • For equal pay and for paying wages, conveyance allowance, house rent allowance, remuneration under an award or settlement, and overtime allowance are counted as wages s. 2(y), second proviso.
  • Remuneration in kind counts as wages up to fifteen per cent of the total wages s. 2(y), Explanation.
  • The Code on Social Security defines wages in the same way, with the same rule on excluded payments above one-half s. 2(88).
  • Gratuity, for example, is worked out on the rate of wages last drawn s. 53(2).

If you are an employer

  • Check each pay component against the list of what wages include and leave out, and add back any excluded payments above one-half of total remuneration s. 2(y).

If you are a worker

  • Allowances that make up most of your pay cannot all be left out of your wages: the part of the excluded payments above one-half of your remuneration counts as wages s. 2(y), first proviso.

An employee is paid ₹20,000 a month as basic pay and dearness allowance, and ₹30,000 a month in allowances the definition leaves out, such as house rent allowance and conveyance allowance.

Check your understanding

6 questions. Choose an answer, then open “Show the answer” to check it against the law's own words.

  1. Question 1. Which of these does the definition of wages in the Code on Wages include?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Dearness allowance

    Wages include basic pay, dearness allowance and any retaining allowance s. 2(y).

  2. Question 2. Which of these does the definition of wages leave out?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Gratuity payable on termination of employment

    Gratuity payable on termination of employment is among the payments the definition of wages leaves out s. 2(y)(j).

  3. Question 3. Excluded payments are added back to wages to the extent they are more than what share of all remuneration?

    Show the answer

    Answer: One-half

    If the excluded payments in clauses (a) to (i) exceed one-half of all remuneration, the amount above one-half is added to wages s. 2(y), first proviso.

  4. Question 4. An employee gets ₹20,000 a month in basic pay and dearness allowance, and ₹30,000 in excluded allowances. What are their wages under the Code?

    Show the answer

    Answer: ₹25,000

    Total remuneration is ₹50,000. Excluded payments above one-half of it, ₹5,000, are added back to the ₹20,000 s. 2(y), first proviso.

  5. Question 5. Up to what share of total wages does remuneration in kind count as wages?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Fifteen per cent

    Remuneration in kind that does not exceed fifteen per cent of the total wages counts as part of the wages s. 2(y), Explanation.

  6. Question 6. For which purposes are house rent allowance and overtime allowance taken into account as wages?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Equal wages for all genders and payment of wages

    For equal wages to all genders and for payment of wages, conveyance allowance, house rent allowance, award remuneration and overtime allowance are taken into account s. 2(y), second proviso.
