Who is covered

Employee and worker, establishment and factory, and the headcounts at which the main parts of the Codes begin.

Written from the official text · not reviewed by a lawyer · follows the Central Rules

Key points

  • Under the Code on Wages an “employee” is anyone employed on wages by an establishment to do skilled, semi-skilled or unskilled, manual, operational, supervisory, managerial, administrative, technical or clerical work, other than an apprentice and members of the Armed Forces s. 2(k).
  • A “worker” is a narrower group: people employed to do manual, unskilled, skilled, technical, operational, clerical or supervisory work s. 2(z). Under the OSH Code, a person employed mainly in a managerial or administrative capacity is not a worker, nor is a supervisor drawing wages above eighteen thousand rupees a month or the amount the Central Government notifies s. 2(1)(zzl).
  • Under the OSH Code, a place where any industry, trade, business, manufacturing or occupation is carried on is an “establishment” if ten or more workers are employed in it s. 2(1)(v).
  • A “factory” is premises where twenty or more workers work with the aid of power, or forty or more without it, counting any day of the preceding twelve months s. 2(1)(w).
  • The Employees’ Provident Fund Chapter applies to every establishment in which twenty or more employees are employed, and the Employees’ State Insurance Chapter to every establishment in which ten or more persons are employed, other than a seasonal factory First Schedule.
  • Gratuity applies to every factory, mine, oilfield, plantation, port and railway company, and to every shop or establishment in which ten or more employees are employed First Schedule.
  • Every industrial establishment employing twenty or more workers must have a Grievance Redressal Committee s. 4(1), and the bonus Chapter applies where twenty or more persons are employed on any day of an accounting year s. 41(2).

If you are an employer

  • Count everyone you employ, and look back over the preceding twelve months: a factory counts the workers who were working on any day in that time s. 2(1)(w).

If you are a worker

  • The minimum wage protects every employee, whatever their job or grade s. 5.

Check your understanding

7 questions. Choose an answer, then open “Show the answer” to check it against the law's own words.

  1. Question 1. Under the OSH Code, a place where a trade or business is carried on is an “establishment” when how many workers are employed in it?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Ten or more

    A place where any industry, trade, business, manufacturing or occupation is carried on is an establishment if ten or more workers are employed s. 2(1)(v).

  2. Question 2. A manufacturing process is carried on with the aid of power. From how many workers are the premises a “factory” under the OSH Code?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Twenty or more

    With the aid of power, premises are a factory where twenty or more workers are working, or were working on any day of the preceding twelve months s. 2(1)(w).

  3. Question 3. Under the OSH Code, a person employed in a supervisory capacity is not a “worker” if their wage is more than how much a month, unless the Central Government notifies another amount?

    Show the answer

    Answer: ₹18,000

    The definition of worker leaves out a supervisor drawing wages above eighteen thousand rupees a month or the amount the Central Government notifies s. 2(1)(zzl).

  4. Question 4. The Employees' Provident Fund Chapter of the Code on Social Security applies to every establishment employing how many employees?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Twenty or more

    The Employees’ Provident Fund Chapter applies to every establishment in which twenty or more employees are employed First Schedule.

  5. Question 5. The Employees' State Insurance Chapter applies to every establishment in which how many persons are employed (a seasonal factory aside)?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Ten or more

    The Employees’ State Insurance Chapter applies to every establishment in which ten or more persons are employed, other than a seasonal factory First Schedule.

  6. Question 6. From how many workers must an industrial establishment have a Grievance Redressal Committee?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Twenty or more

    Every industrial establishment employing twenty or more workers must have one or more Grievance Redressal Committees s. 4(1).

  7. Question 7. Whom does the Code on Wages definition of “employee” cover?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Managerial and administrative staff as well as manual and clerical workers

    An employee is anyone employed on wages to do skilled, semi-skilled or unskilled, manual, operational, supervisory, managerial, administrative, technical or clerical work s. 2(k).
