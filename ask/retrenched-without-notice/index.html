Can my employer remove me without notice?

  • When an employer ends your job for a reason other than punishment, that is generally retrenchment. Retirement, the end of a fixed-term contract and continued ill-health are not s. 2(zh).
  • After at least a year of continuous service, you must get a month’s written notice giving the reasons, or wages for that period instead s. 70(a).
  • You must also be paid fifteen days’ average pay for every completed year, counting more than six months as a year s. 70(b).
  • A factory, mine or plantation with at least three hundred workers needs the Government’s prior permission, and must give three months’ notice or wages s. 77(1) s. 79(1).
  • These rules protect workers. A person employed mainly as a manager or administrator, or a supervisor drawing wages above ₹18,000 a month, is not a worker s. 2(zr).

What the Labour Ministry says

  • “Industrial establishments such as factories, mines, and plantations employing 50 to 299 workers must serve notice on appropriate government or such authority as specified by the appropriate government before carrying out any lay-off, retrenchment, or closure.” para 4.5, Compliance Handbook for Employers, Feb 2026: official copy · our copy

    Section 70's retrenchment conditions have no such range: they cover a worker employed in any industry with a year's continuous service. The Code prevails.

This is the Ministry's explanation, not the law. Its FAQs and Handbook say the Codes prevail.
